Option 1: Why is it important to work to delete cyberbullying?
Cyberbullying is easy to start and hard to stop because the tools behind it were built to spread things quickly: a screenshot, a group chat or an anonymous account can reach a whole school in minutes, and it keeps going after the bell rings, at night and on weekends, where no teacher sees it. That's why the work has to happen on more than one front. Students need to know that a trusted adult outside school, like a coach, relative or employer, can help, and they need to be able to report it without being treated as the problem. Platforms carry real responsibility too. I build software, including an AI system that triages city repair requests and hands the risky calls to a person instead of deciding alone, and good systems are designed to notice harm early and route it to someone who can act. Social apps can do the same with faster reporting, private-by-default settings and moderation that doesn't put the whole burden on the person being targeted. Deleting cyberbullying matters because the target usually can't log off from their own life.
